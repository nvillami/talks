Developing feedback laws for high-dimensional nonlinear dynamics through efficient data-driven approaches, guaranteeing robustness, real-time computability, and sparse control action .

The Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman PDE in control

\[ \begin{aligned} &\underset{\mathbf u(\cdot)\in\mathcal U}{\text{minimize}} &&\hspace{-0.5cm} \mathcal J(\mathbf u(\cdot);\mathbf x) = \int_0^\infty \ell(\mathbf y(t)) +\|\mathbf u(t)\|_R^2 \,dt \\ &\text{subject to} &&\hspace{-0.5cm} \dot{\mathbf y}(t) = \mathbf f(\mathbf y(t)) + \mathbf g(\mathbf y(t))\mathbf u(t), \; \mathbf y(0)=\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d . \end{aligned} \]

Dynamic Programming (Bellman 1950s): the value function (optimal-cost-to-go)

$ V(\mathbf x) := \inf_{\mathbf u(\cdot)\in\mathcal U} \mathcal J(\mathbf u;\mathbf x), \qquad \mathcal U \equiv L^\infty([0,+\infty);U), $

satisfies the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation

$ \underset{\mathbf u\in U}{\inf} \left[ \nabla V(\mathbf x)^\top (\mathbf f(\mathbf x)+\mathbf g(\mathbf x)\mathbf u) + \ell(\mathbf x) + \|\mathbf u\|_R^2 \right] =0, \qquad \mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d . $

The optimal control is a feedback map:

$ \mathbf u^*(\mathbf x(t)) := \operatorname*{argmin}_{\mathbf u\in U} \left[ \nabla V(\mathbf x)^\top (\mathbf g(\mathbf x)\mathbf u) + \|\mathbf u\|_R^2 \right]$

W. E. The Dawning of a New Era in Applied Mathematics. Notices of the AMS, April 2021.

High-dimensional HJB PDEs and Machine Learning

Supervised learning:

  • Representation formulas (Osher-Darbon 16', Kang-Wilcox 17')
  • Polynomial regression + LASSO (Azmi et al. 21', Haasdonk/Ehring 23'), deterministic,
  • Deep neural networks (Nakamura-Zimmerer et al. 19', Bicego et al. 21'),
  • Feedback training directly from optimality-based loss (Kunisch and Walter 20'),
  • Data-driven tensor decompositions (Dolgov et. al 22'),
  • Separable representations for value functions (Saluzzi/Sperl et al. 25'),
  • Bang-bang theorems in time-optimal control as binary classification (Bicego/Gue et al. 25').

Semi/un-supervised learning, RL:

  • Deep BSDE solver (E-Han-Jentzen 17'), stochastic control, Feynman-Kac formula,
  • Deep Galerkin Method (Sirignano-Spiliopoulos 18'), stochastic control, residual minimization,
  • ML for MFG and MFOC (Laurière et al. 21', Ruthotto et al. 21'),
  • LS-FD with DNNs (Esteve-Yagüe et al. 25'),

Methodological boundaries:

  • $\Rightarrow$ Constrained vs. unconstrained state/control: impacts the smoothness of the value function.
  • $\Rightarrow$ ODE vs PDE-type nonlinearites: $\mathbf f(\mathbf x)$ affects the shape of the value function.
  • $\Rightarrow$ Linear vs non-linear regression: optimization framework.

Finite-dimensional multi-level optimal control

Linear-quadratic parameter dependent optimal control problem: $ \begin{aligned} &\min_u\;J(u;z_0,r) = \int_0^\infty z(t)^\top Qz(t)+u(t)^\top Ru(t)\,dt,\\ &\text{subject to:}\qquad \frac{dz}{dt}=Az(t)+B(r)u(t), \quad z(0)=z_0, \qquad A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n},\; B(r)\in\mathbb R^{n\times m}. \end{aligned} $

Actuator design - robustness - performance

$ \min_{r\in\Omega^m} \max_{\substack{z_0\in\mathbb R^n\\ \|z_0\|=1}} \min_{u\in\mathcal U}J(u;z_0,r) = \min_{r\in\Omega^m} \max_{\substack{z_0\in\mathbb R^n\\ \|z_0\|=1}} V(z_0,r). $

Parameter-dependent optimal cost-to-go:

$ V(z_0,r)\colon = \min_{u\in\mathcal U}J(u;z_0,r) = z_0^\top \Pi(r)z_0. $

Characterization of the lower-level problem (ARE):

$ A^\top\Pi(r)+\Pi(r)A -\Pi(r)B(r)R^{-1}B(r)^\top\Pi(r)+Q=0. $

D.K., E. Loayza-Romero, K. Morris and Z. Zhong, Multi-level Optimal Control with Neural Surrogate Models, MTNS 2024.

PINNs for infinite horizon HJB

Residual minimization and DNNs for solving HJB (DGM): $$ \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{res}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \|\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}^i,V_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}^i))\|_2^2, \qquad \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},V) := \nabla V(\mathbf{x})^{\top}\mathbf f(\mathbf{x}) -\frac14\nabla V(\mathbf{x})^{\top}\mathbf g(\mathbf{x})\mathbf R^{-1}\mathbf g(\mathbf{x})^{\top}\nabla V(\mathbf{x}) +\ell(\mathbf{x}). $$

Minimizing the HJB PDE residual does not guarantee convergence to the value function !

Residual + data loss: $$ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{dat+res}}(\theta) =& \frac{1}{N_1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_1} \|\bar V(\mathbf{x}^i)-V_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}^i)\|_2^2\\ &+ \frac{\lambda_1}{N_1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_1} \|\nabla \bar V(\mathbf{x}^i)-\nabla V_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}^i)\|_2^2\\ &+ \frac{\lambda_2}{N_2}\sum_{i=1}^{N_2} \|\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}^i,V_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}^i))\|_2^2. \end{aligned} $$

Needs to fit inaccurate data.

Pre-trained PINNs with supervised learning

Supervised phase: $$ \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{dat}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N_1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_1} \|\bar V(\mathbf{x}^i)-V_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}^i)\|_2^2 + \frac{\lambda_1}{N_1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_1} \|\nabla \bar V(\mathbf{x}^i)-\nabla V_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}^i)\|_2^2. $$ Residual phase initialized with supervised phase parameters: $$ \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{res}}(\theta) = \frac{\lambda_2}{N_2}\sum_{i=1}^{N_2} \|\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}^i,V_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}^i))\|_2^2. $$

A. Borovykh, D. K., A. Laignelet and P. Parpas. Data-driven initialization of deep learning solvers for Hamilton-Jacobi PDEs, IFAC-PapersOnline, 2022.

Model+data-driven dynamic optimization

A concrete example:

Lax-Hopf formula (Osher-Darbon 16')

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{\partial V(\mathbf{x},t)}{\partial t} &+ \frac12\|\nabla_x V(\mathbf{x},t)\|^2=0,\\ V(\mathbf{x},0)&=\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{x}),\\ V(\mathbf{x},t) &= -\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^d} \left\{ \mathcal{J}^*(\mathbf{y}) + \frac{t}{2}\|\mathbf{y}\|^2 - \langle \mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle \right\}. \end{aligned} $$
  • Convex optimization problem.
  • Solvable in real-time.
  • $\nabla V$ can be computed for free.
  • What is the counterpart for relevant control problems?

J. Darbon and S. Osher, Algorithms for Overcoming the Curse of Dimensionality for Certain Hamilton-Jacobi Equations Arising in Control Theory and Elsewhere, Res. Math. Sci. 2016.

Finite horizon optimal control

$$ \begin{aligned} \min_{u(\cdot)\in L^2(t_0,T;\mathbb R^m)} \mathcal{J}(\mathbf u;t_0,\mathbf x) &:= \int_{t_0}^{T} \ell(\mathbf y(t))+\beta\|\mathbf u(t)\|_2^2\,dt, \qquad \beta>0,\\ \text{subject to}\qquad \frac{d}{dt}\mathbf y(t) &= \mathbf f(\mathbf y(t))+\mathbf g(\mathbf y(t))\mathbf u(t), \qquad \mathbf y(t_0)=\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d. \end{aligned} $$
  • Optimal feedback law: $\mathbf u^*(t,\mathbf x)= -\frac{1}{2\beta}\mathbf g^{\top}(\mathbf x)\nabla V(t,\mathbf x)$, where $V(t,\mathbf x):[0,T]\times\mathbb R^d\Rightarrow\mathbb R$ solves $$ \partial_t V(t,\mathbf x) - \frac{1}{4\beta} \nabla V(t,\mathbf x)^{\top} \mathbf g(\mathbf x)\mathbf g^{\top}(\mathbf x) \nabla V(t,\mathbf x) + \nabla V(t,\mathbf x)^{\top}\mathbf f(\mathbf x) + \ell(\mathbf x)=0, \quad V(T,\mathbf x)=0. $$
  • Pontryagin's Maximum Principle for a single trajectory departing from $\mathbf y(t_0)=\mathbf x$: $$ \tag{TPBVP} \left\{ \begin{aligned} \dot{\mathbf y}(t) &= \mathbf f(\mathbf y(t))+\mathbf g(\mathbf y(t))\mathbf u(t), & \mathbf y(t_0)&=\mathbf x,\\ -\dot{\mathbf p}(t) &= \nabla_{\mathbf y} (\mathbf f(\mathbf y(t))+\mathbf g(\mathbf y(t))\mathbf u(t))^{\top}\mathbf p(t) + \nabla_{\mathbf y}\ell(\mathbf y(t)), & \mathbf p(T)&=0,\\ \mathbf u(t) &= -\frac{1}{2\beta}\mathbf g^{\top}(\mathbf y(t))\mathbf p(t), & &\forall t\in(t_0,T). \end{aligned} \right. $$

The link between HJB and PMP

Theorem (Mirică 85', Subbotina 06', Yegorov and Dower 17'). Let $\mathbf f$, $\mathbf g$ and $\ell$ be $\mathcal C^1(\mathbb R^d)$. Then, the characteristic curves of the HJB PDE $$ \partial_t V(t,\mathbf x) - \frac{1}{4\beta} \nabla V(t,\mathbf x)^{\top}\mathbf g(\mathbf x)\mathbf g^{\top}(\mathbf x) \nabla V(t,\mathbf x) + \nabla V(t,\mathbf x)^{\top}\mathbf f(\mathbf x) + \ell(\mathbf x)=0, \quad V(T,\mathbf x)=0, $$ correspond to the solution of the TPBVP departing from $\mathbf y(t_0)=\mathbf x$: $$ \tag{TPBVP} \left\{ \begin{aligned} \dot{\mathbf y}(t) &= \mathbf f(\mathbf y(t))+\mathbf g(\mathbf y(t))\mathbf u(t), & \mathbf y(t_0)&=\mathbf x,\\ -\dot{\mathbf p}(t) &= \nabla_{\mathbf y} (\mathbf f(\mathbf y(t))+\mathbf g(\mathbf y(t))\mathbf u(t))^{\top}\mathbf p(t) + \nabla_{\mathbf y}\ell(\mathbf y(t)), & \mathbf p(T)&=0,\\ \mathbf u(t) &= -\frac{1}{2\beta}\mathbf g^{\top}(\mathbf y(t))\mathbf p(t), & &\forall t\in(t_0,T). \end{aligned} \right. $$ Moreover, along an optimal trajectory $(\mathbf y^*(t),\mathbf p^*(t),\mathbf u^*(t);\mathbf x)$, the value function and its gradient satisfy $$ V(t,\mathbf y^*(t)) = \int_t^T \ell(\mathbf y^*(s))+\beta\|\mathbf u^*(s)\|_2^2\,ds, \quad \nabla V(t,\mathbf y^*(t))=\mathbf p^*(t), \quad \forall t\in(t_0,T). $$

N. N. Subbotina. The method of characteristics for the Hamilton-Jacobi equations and its applications in dynamic optimization. J. Math. Sci., 2006.

Generating a synthetic dataset for regression

  • Sampling a set of $N_s$ initial conditions $\mathbf y(0)=\mathbf x_i,\ i=1,\ldots,N_s$ from a high-dimensional pdf.
  • Numerical realization of PMP for each $\mathbf x_i$: TPBVP, reduced gradient or Newton.
  • Dataset: $\{(\mathbf x_i,\mathbf y_i^*(t),\mathbf p_i^*(t),\mathbf u_i^*(t))\}_{i=1}^{N_s}$. Compute $V(0,\mathbf x_i)=\mathcal J(\mathbf u_i^*(t);0,\mathbf x_i)$ and $\nabla V(0,\mathbf x_i)=\mathbf p_i^*(0)$.

Polynomial models for value function approximation

  • There is a extensive (and rigorous) literature on power series expansions for approximating the solution of HJB PDEs, dating back to Al’brekht 61' until Breiten et al. 19'. Interpretability?
  • In the LQ case, $V(\mathbf x)=\mathbf x^{\top}\Pi\mathbf x$, exact recovery with quadratic polynomials.
  • Approximation subspace from tensorization of 1d basis: monomial, Chebyshev, or Legendre.
  • Sample complexity estimates (Adcock and Sui 19'): $$ \| f-\hat f \|_{\tilde H^1(D)} \lesssim \underbrace{\| f-f_{\Lambda} \|_{\tilde H^1(D)}}_{\text{Projection error}} + \underbrace{\| x_{\Lambda}-x_{\Delta} \|_{1,v}}_{\text{Sparsity error}} + \underbrace{\sqrt{|\Delta|_w}\eta}_{\text{Measurement noise}}. $$ with probability $1-\epsilon$ for $m$ function-gradient samples such that $m\gtrapprox s\log(\epsilon^{-1})\log(2d)\log(2s)$.

B. Adcock and Y. Sui, Compressive Hermite Interpolation: Sparse, High-Dimensional Approximation from Gradient-Augmented Measurements, Constr. Approx. 2019.

Gradient-augmented regression

  • Augmented data $D_{aug}=\left\lbrace \mathbf{x}^j,V^j,V_x^j\right\rbrace^{N_d}_{j=1}$, with $V^j_x=\left( \frac{\partial V}{\partial x_1}(\mathbf{x}^j), \frac{\partial V}{\partial x_1}(\mathbf{x}^j), \dots, \frac{\partial V}{\partial x_n}(\mathbf{x}^j) \right)^{\top}$.
  • Fitting $\theta$ with linear least squares and LASSO regression: $$ \bar{\theta}_{\ell_2} = \underset{\theta\in\mathbb R^q}{\operatorname{argmin}} \|\bar{\mathbf A}\theta-\bar{\mathbf V}\|_2^2, \quad \bar{\theta}_{\ell_1} = \underset{\theta\in\mathbb R^q}{\operatorname{argmin}} \|\bar{\mathbf A}\theta-\bar{\mathbf V}\|_2^2 + \lambda\|\theta\|_{1,\mathbf w}, $$ with the recovered feedback law $\mathbf u_{\theta}(\mathbf x)= -\frac{1}{2\beta}\mathbf g^{\top} \sum_{i=1}^q\theta_i\nabla_x\Phi_i(\mathbf x)$.
  • Linear least squares matrix assembly: $$ \mathbf A_0:= \left(\Phi_k(\mathbf x^j)\right)^{N_d,q}_{j,k=1}, \quad \mathbf A_m:= \left( \frac{\partial\Phi_k}{\partial x_m}(\mathbf x^j) \right)^{N_d,q}_{j,k=1}, \; \mathbf V_0:= \left(V(\mathbf x^j)\right)^{N_d}_{j=1}, \quad \mathbf V_m:= \left( \frac{\partial V}{\partial x_m}(\mathbf x^j) \right)^{N_d}_{j=1}, $$ and $$ \bar{\mathbf A} := \frac{1}{\sqrt{N_d}} \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf A_0 & \mathbf A_1 & \ldots & \mathbf A_m \end{pmatrix}^{\top}, \qquad \bar{\mathbf V} := \frac{1}{\sqrt{N_d}} \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf V_0 & \mathbf V_1 & \ldots & \mathbf V_m \end{pmatrix}^{\top}. $$

Sobolev training with second-order information

$$ \mathcal L(V,V_{\theta}) := \frac{1}{N_s}\sum_{i=1}^{N_s} \|V(\mathbf x^{(i)})-V_{\theta}(\mathbf x^{(i)})\|^2 + \mu_1\|\nabla V(\mathbf x^{(i)})-\nabla V_{\theta}(\mathbf x^{(i)})\|^2 + \mu_2\|H(V(\mathbf x^{(i)}))-H(V_{\theta}(\mathbf x^{(i)}))\|_F^2. $$ $$ \text{(HJB)} \begin{cases} \partial_t V(t,\mathbf x)+\mathcal H(\mathbf x,\nabla V(t,\mathbf x))=0, \quad \text{for } t\in(0,T),\;\; V(T,\mathbf x)=0,\\ \mathcal H(\mathbf x,\mathbf p) := \min_{u\in\mathbb R^m} \left\{ \ell(\mathbf x)+\beta\|\mathbf u\|_2^2 + \mathbf p^{\top} \left(\mathbf f(\mathbf x)+\mathbf g(\mathbf x)\mathbf u\right) \right\}. \end{cases} $$ Assuming that the $V(t,\mathbf x)$ is twice Fréchet in $[0,T]$, its Hessian $\Pi(t):=-\mathbf H(V(t,\mathbf x))$ is given by: $$ \partial_t\Pi(t) + \mathcal H_{\mathbf p\mathbf x}(t)\Pi(t) + \Pi(t)\mathcal H_{\mathbf x\mathbf p} + \Pi(t)\mathcal H_{\mathbf p\mathbf p}(t)\Pi(t) + \mathcal H_{\mathbf x\mathbf x}(t) = 0, \quad \Pi(T)=0, $$ where $\mathcal H_{\mathbf p\mathbf x}(t):=\mathcal H(\mathbf x^*(t),\mathbf p^*(t))$.
  • Adds $d^2/2$ data per sample.
  • Brings curvature information.
  • Expensive to compute (stiffness?).
  • Errors to be measured in $H^2$ seminorm:
$$ \begin{split} Err_{H^2}(\bar V) := \left( \frac{ \displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal I_{val}} \left( \displaystyle\sum_{i,k=1}^{n} \left| \frac{\partial^2\bar V(\mathbf x^j)}{\partial x_k\partial x_i} - \frac{\partial^2 V(0,\mathbf x^j)}{\partial x_k\partial x_i} \right|^2 \right) }{ \displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal I_{val}} \left( \displaystyle\sum_{i,k=1}^{n} \left| \frac{\partial^2 V(0,\mathbf x^j)}{\partial x_k\partial x_i} \right|^2 \right) } \right)^{1/2}. \end{split} $$

Adaptive basis selection: a closed-form residual-decay indicator

Approximation $\hat V^{(k)}\in \mathrm{span}\{\phi_j:j\in\Gamma^{(k)}\}$ Residual $R(\hat V^{(k)}):=\mathcal L_{\mathrm HJB}\hat V^{(k)}$

Which $\phi_j$ to add next?

Single-direction projected DWR.

$$ e^{(k)}_{j,\mathrm{PDE}} = \frac{ \left| \langle R(\hat V^{(k)}), a_k^\top\nabla\phi_j \rangle_{L^2} \right| }{ \|a_k^\top\nabla\phi_j\|_{L^2} } $$ $$ a_k(\mathbf x) = \mathbf f(\mathbf x) - \frac1{2\beta} \mathbf g(\mathbf x)\mathbf g(\mathbf x)^\top \nabla\hat V^{(k)}(\mathbf x) $$

Closed-form, mesh-free, dimension-agnostic.

  • Greedy enrichment: $j^*=\arg\max_{j\in\Lambda^{(k)}} |e^{(k)}_{j,\mathrm{PDE}}|$
  • Empirical version $\tilde e^{(k)}_{j,\mathrm{PDE}}$ from supervised samples.
  • Joint data selection via gradient-augmented score.
